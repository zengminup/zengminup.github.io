## Abstract

Neuroblastoma is a pediatric solid tumor characterized by remarkable cellular heterogeneity and phenotypic plasticity, which drive therapy resistance and relapse. The adrenergic (ADRN) and mesenchymal (MES) cell states represent two major transcriptional programs that interconvert through a plasticity continuum. Here, we integrate single-cell RNA-seq, spatial transcriptomics, and bulk ATAC-seq/H3K27ac data to examine the molecular mechanisms underlying ADRN/MES plasticity, pseudotime trajectories, and epigenetic regulation in neuroblastoma. We highlight DLK1 as a pseudotime-associated immunotherapeutic target and discuss the role of MYCN amplification and super-enhancers in shaping the epigenetic landscape of high-risk disease.

## 1. Introduction

Neuroblastoma arises from neural crest–derived sympathoadrenal progenitors and accounts for approximately 15% of pediatric cancer deaths. The disease exhibits striking clinical heterogeneity, ranging from spontaneous regression to aggressive metastatic disease. The ADRN and MES cell states—initially defined by *PHOX2B* and *PRRX1* expression, respectively—represent the extremes of a plasticity continuum that contributes to tumor heterogeneity and drug resistance. Understanding the molecular and epigenetic mechanisms governing this plasticity is essential for developing novel therapeutic strategies.

## 2. Single-Cell Landscape and Pseudotime Trajectories

### 2.1 Cellular Composition of Neuroblastoma

Single-cell RNA-seq of neuroblastoma tumors (EGAD00001008345; 13,281 cells from 16 patients) resolves major cell populations including tumor clusters, ambiguous populations, Schwannian stroma, endothelium, mesenchyme, and leukocytes. UMAP visualization and annotation reveal the coexistence of ADRN-like and MES-like tumor cells within individual tumors.

**Single-Cell Annotation and Pseudotime Trajectory of Neuroblastoma**<br/><img src="/images/Neuroblastoma/Neuroblastoma_2.png"><br/>

### 2.2 Key Transcription Factors Along Pseudotime

Pseudotime analysis reveals dynamic expression of key neuroblastoma transcription factors, including *ASCL1*, *GATA3*, *HAND2*, *MYCN*, and *PHOX2B*. *MYCN* exhibits a transient peak along the trajectory, while *GATA3* and *HAND2* show sustained expression, consistent with their roles in maintaining the adrenergic state.

## 3. DLK1 as a Pseudotime-Associated Immunotherapeutic Target

### 3.1 Pseudotime-Associated Gene Testing

Pseudotime-associated gene testing identifies *DLK1* as a top-ranked gene whose expression correlates with the differentiation trajectory. DLK1 (delta-like 1 homolog) is a Notch ligand and a surface protein that has emerged as a promising immunotherapeutic target in neuroblastoma.

**Pseudotime-Associated Gene Testing Identifies DLK1**<br/><img src="/images/Neuroblastoma/Neuroblastoma_3.png"><br/>

### 3.2 ADRN vs MES Marker Gene Expression

Dot plot analysis of ADRN and MES marker genes across tumor subclusters reveals that *PHOX2B*, *GATA3*, *HAND2*, and *ISL1* mark the ADRN state, while *PRRX1*, *VIM*, *SNAI2*, and *SOX9* mark the MES state. DLK1 expression is enriched in specific subclusters, supporting its potential as a therapeutic target.

## 4. Spatial Transcriptomics of Neuroblastoma

### 4.1 snRNA-seq of Human Adrenal Gland Neuroblastoma

Single-nucleus RNA-seq of human adrenal gland neuroblastoma (372,619 cells; *Nat Genet* 2025) resolves major cell types including neuroblasts, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and macrophages. Cell type composition analysis reveals that neuroblasts constitute the majority of cells in both control and treated conditions, with treated tumors showing a relative expansion of neuroblasts.

### 4.2 Xenium TH-MYCN Mouse Model

Xenium in situ spatial transcriptomics of TH-MYCN mice (*Nat Genet* 2025) reveals the spatial organization of neuroblastoma cells. Spatial pseudotime trajectory analysis identifies distinct spatial domains corresponding to different differentiation states, with neuroblasts occupying the tumor core and invasive fronts marked by distinct molecular signatures.

## 5. Epigenetic Regulation of Neuroblastoma Heterogeneity

### 5.1 Bulk ATAC-seq and H3K27ac Profiling

Bulk ATAC-seq and H3K27ac ChIP-seq (GSE136209) of ADRN/MES neuroblastoma cell lines reveal extensive chromatin remodeling across cell states. H3K27ac tracks at the *LMX1B* and *PHOX2B* loci demonstrate differential enhancer activity between ADRN and MES states, consistent with state-specific transcriptional programs.

### 5.2 Transcription Factor Networks and MYCN Status

Network analysis of transcription factors identifies key regulators of ADRN/MES heterogeneity, including *SOX5*, *NR2F2*, *HAND2*, *PHOX2B*, *PRRX1*, and *LMX1B*. These factors form a densely interconnected regulatory network that integrates MYCN status and metastatic potential.

**Bulk ATAC-seq and H3K27ac Reveal State-Specific Chromatin Landscapes**<br/><img src="/images/Neuroblastoma/Neuroblastoma_6.png"><br/>

## 6. Conclusion

Integration of single-cell, spatial, and epigenomic data reveals a multilayered landscape of neuroblastoma plasticity and heterogeneity. ADRN/MES interconversion is governed by a core transcription factor network, with DLK1 emerging as a pseudotime-associated immunotherapeutic target. Spatial transcriptomics of TH-MYCN mice and snRNA-seq of human tumors provide complementary views of tumor architecture, while bulk ATAC-seq and H3K27ac profiling reveal state-specific chromatin landscapes. These findings support the development of therapies targeting neuroblastoma plasticity and its epigenetic drivers.
