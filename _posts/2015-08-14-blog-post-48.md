## Abstract

*Helicobacter pylori* is a major etiological factor in gastric carcinogenesis, with the cytotoxin-associated gene A (CagA) effector protein contributing to host–pathogen interactions and gastric epithelial remodeling. Here, we integrate bulk RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from gastric epithelial cell models with single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from human gastric biopsies (GSE249874) to characterize transcriptional responses and cellular heterogeneity associated with *H. pylori* infection. In vitro analyses identify differential gene expression patterns associated with CagA status, including changes in *CLDN2* and *CXCL8* expression under the examined experimental conditions. Complementary single-cell profiling of samples from 18 patients resolves major epithelial, immune, and stromal populations and reveals substantial inter-individual variation in cellular composition across *H. pylori* status. Together, these analyses illustrate the complementary value of bulk and single-cell transcriptomics for investigating host responses to *H. pylori* and generating hypotheses regarding the cellular mechanisms underlying gastric pathology.

## 1. Introduction

*H. pylori* is a widespread gastric pathogen associated with chronic gastritis, peptic ulcer disease, and gastric adenocarcinoma. Its classification as a Group 1 carcinogen reflects the established causal relationship between chronic infection and gastric cancer development. Among its virulence factors, CagA is delivered into host gastric epithelial cells through the bacterial type IV secretion system, where it can perturb intracellular signaling, epithelial polarity, intercellular junctions, and inflammatory responses.

Characterizing the molecular and cellular consequences of *H. pylori* infection requires approaches that capture both transcriptional responses within experimental models and the cellular complexity of human gastric tissue. Bulk RNA-seq enables the characterization of population-level gene expression changes, whereas scRNA-seq resolves transcriptional heterogeneity across individual cell populations. Integrating these complementary approaches provides a framework for investigating CagA-associated epithelial responses and the broader tissue-level changes accompanying *H. pylori* infection.

## 2. CagA-Associated Transcriptional Responses In Vitro

### 2.1. Comparison of Wild-Type and CagA-Knockout *H. pylori*

Bulk RNA-seq analysis comparing wild-type (WT) and CagA-knockout (KO) *H. pylori* conditions identifies distinct gene expression patterns in the examined experimental system. The heatmap of selected genes illustrates differences in the expression profiles of bacterial-associated genes, including *dupA*, *ureA*, *fliD*, and *cagA*. Notably, *cagA* and *ureB* display differential patterns between the WT and KO conditions.

These observations provide an initial overview of transcriptional differences associated with CagA status. Interpretation of individual gene-level changes should account for the experimental design, the origin of the sequenced RNA, and the distinction between bacterial transcriptional profiles and host epithelial responses.

### 2.2. Time-Dependent Transcriptional Changes in GES-1 Cells

To investigate temporal variation in gastric epithelial transcriptional responses, RNA-seq data from GES-1 cells under CagA-KO-associated experimental conditions were examined at 12 and 24 hours (GSE243405). Selected genes, including *CLDN2* and *CXCL8*, exhibit increased expression at the examined time points.

*CLDN2* encodes claudin-2, a tight-junction-associated protein involved in epithelial paracellular permeability, whereas *CXCL8* encodes the pro-inflammatory chemokine CXCL8/IL-8, which can promote neutrophil recruitment. Their expression patterns therefore highlight epithelial barrier regulation and inflammatory signaling as relevant processes for further investigation. However, the direction and magnitude of these responses should be interpreted in the context of the specific experimental comparisons, rather than attributed exclusively to the presence or absence of CagA without appropriate controls.

## 3. Single-Cell Transcriptomic Landscape of Human Gastric Mucosa

### 3.1. Cell-Type Annotation and Marker Gene Expression

Single-cell RNA-seq data from human gastric biopsies (GSE249874; 18 patients) were used to characterize the cellular composition of the gastric mucosa. Major annotated populations include epithelial cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, myeloid cells, B cells, plasma cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, and other lymphocyte populations.

Marker gene expression supports the annotation of these major cell populations. *EPCAM* is associated with epithelial cells, *PECAM1* with endothelial cells, *ACTA2* with smooth muscle and other contractile cell populations, and *CD3E* with T lymphocytes. The combined expression patterns of these markers provide a basis for distinguishing major cellular compartments and interpreting downstream analyses of tissue heterogeneity.

### 3.2. Feature Plots of Canonical Cell-Type Markers

Feature plots projected onto the UMAP embedding provide an additional assessment of cell-type annotation by visualizing the distribution of canonical marker genes. *EPCAM* marks epithelial populations, whereas *PECAM1* highlights endothelial cells. *ACTA2* identifies smooth muscle and other contractile populations, while *CD3E*, *CD4*, and *CD8A* help characterize T-cell populations.

Additional markers delineate immune cell heterogeneity: *CD14* and *CD68* are associated with myeloid populations; *HLA-DRA* indicates major histocompatibility complex class II-associated antigen presentation; *MS4A1* marks B cells; *JCHAIN* is enriched in antibody-secreting plasma cells; *S100A8* is associated with inflammatory myeloid populations; and *CPA3* supports the identification of mast cells.

The distribution of these markers across the embedding provides complementary evidence for the annotated cell identities. Nevertheless, individual markers are not necessarily exclusive to a single cell type, and robust annotation benefits from evaluating multiple markers together with the broader transcriptional profile.

### 3.3. UMAP-Based Visualization of Gastric Cell Populations

UMAP visualization resolves ten major annotated cellular populations: B cells, CD4+ T cells, CD8+ T cells, endothelial cells, epithelial cells, fibroblasts, myeloid cells, other lymphocytes, plasma cells, and smooth muscle cells. The distribution of these populations reveals distinct transcriptional neighborhoods, reflecting differences in their underlying gene expression profiles.

Although the annotated populations occupy distinguishable regions of the embedding, UMAP separation should be interpreted as a visualization of transcriptional similarity rather than definitive evidence of discrete biological boundaries. Closely related populations may share transcriptional programs, and their apparent separation can depend on data processing, dimensionality reduction, and clustering parameters.

## 4. Inter-Individual Heterogeneity and *H. pylori* Status

### 4.1. UMAP Visualization Across Patient Conditions

To explore inter-individual variation, UMAP embeddings were examined across patients stratified by *H. pylori* status (HP− and HP+). The visualizations reveal variation in the representation of epithelial and immune populations among individual patients, with some samples showing differences in the apparent distribution of specific cellular compartments.

Plasma cell and T-cell populations exhibit notable variability across individuals, highlighting the heterogeneous nature of the gastric mucosal immune landscape. These differences may reflect variation in infection-associated immune responses, disease characteristics, sampling, and other patient-specific factors. Given the observational nature of the dataset, the visualized patterns should be interpreted as descriptive associations rather than direct evidence of infection-induced cellular changes.

**UMAP Embeddings Across Patients Stratified by *H. pylori* Status**<br/><img src="/images/HP/HP_5.png"><br/>

### 4.2. Patient-Level Cell-Type Composition

Stacked bar plots were used to compare the relative proportions of annotated cell types across patients and *H. pylori* status. The results illustrate substantial inter-individual variation in the cellular composition of gastric biopsies, including differences in the relative representation of epithelial, plasma cell, T-cell, and myeloid compartments.

Variation in plasma cell and T-cell proportions among HP+ samples may be consistent with differences in mucosal immune activation associated with infection. However, cell-type proportions alone cannot establish increased absolute cell numbers or tissue infiltration, and observed differences may also reflect variation in biopsy composition, sampling depth, and patient characteristics. Formal statistical comparisons accounting for the patient-level sampling structure would be required to establish robust associations between *H. pylori* status and specific cell populations.

## 5. Conclusion

This integrated analysis combines bulk and single-cell transcriptomic data to characterize CagA-associated gene expression patterns and cellular heterogeneity in *H. pylori*-related gastric pathology. In vitro analyses highlight transcriptional changes involving genes associated with epithelial barrier regulation and inflammatory signaling, including *CLDN2* and *CXCL8*. Complementary scRNA-seq profiling of gastric biopsies from 18 patients resolves major epithelial, immune, and stromal populations and demonstrates substantial inter-individual variation in cellular composition across *H. pylori* status.

Together, these findings establish a descriptive framework for investigating host transcriptional responses and tissue-level heterogeneity in *H. pylori* infection. Further analyses incorporating appropriately controlled experimental comparisons, patient-level statistical modeling, and independent validation will be important for distinguishing CagA-specific effects from broader infection-associated responses and for determining the biological significance of the observed cellular differences.
