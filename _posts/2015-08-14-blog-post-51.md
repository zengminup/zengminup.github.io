## Abstract

Regulatory T cells (Tregs) are essential for maintaining peripheral tolerance and preventing autoimmunity. Their function depends on a complex interplay of transcription factors, chromatin architecture, and T cell receptor (TCR) signaling. Here, we integrate bulk RNA-seq, ATAC-seq, Hi-C, and single-cell TCR profiling data to examine the transcriptional and epigenetic regulation of Tregs in intestinal tolerance and autoimmune diabetes. We highlight the critical role of c-Maf in establishing the intestinal Treg regulatory landscape, the contribution of CTCF-mediated 3D genome organization to Treg stability, and the clonal expansion of T cell subsets across tissues in NOD mice.

## 1. Introduction

Tregs are a specialized subset of CD4+ T cells that suppress immune responses and maintain self-tolerance. Their development and function are governed by the transcription factor Foxp3, but additional factors—including c-Maf—are required for tissue-specific Treg programs. In the intestine, Tregs adapt to the local microenvironment and acquire unique transcriptional and epigenetic states. Disruption of these programs can lead to autoimmunity, as observed in NOD mice. Recent multi-omic studies provide a high-resolution view of Treg regulation across tissues.

## 2. c-Maf-Dependent Transcriptional and Epigenetic Programs in Intestinal Tregs

### 2.1 Chromatin Accessibility and Gene Expression in Maf△Treg

*Nature Immunology* (2019) identified direct c-Maf target gene candidates in gut-associated Tregs and characterized the transcriptional and chromatin changes following c-Maf deletion. Heatmap analysis of differentially expressed genes and ATAC peaks in lamina propria (LP), Peyer's patches (PPs), and Maf△Treg reveals that c-Maf regulates key genes including *Il10*, *Rorc*, *Ctla4*, *Cxcr5*, *Pdcd1*, *Pik3cd*, and *Sh2d1a*. These results highlight c-Maf's critical role in establishing the regulatory landscape of intestinal Tregs.

### 2.2 Chromatin Accessibility at Ctla4 and Il17a Loci

ATAC-seq tracks at the *Ctla4* and *Il17a* loci demonstrate that c-Maf deletion alters chromatin accessibility at these key regulatory regions. c-Maf ChIP-seq in Th17 cells and ATAC-seq in control and Maf△Treg reveal reduced accessibility at *Ctla4* and increased accessibility at *Il17a*, consistent with a shift toward a pro-inflammatory phenotype.

**Chromatin Accessibility at Ctla4 and Il17a Loci in Intestinal Maf△Treg**<br/><img src="/images/Treg/Treg_2.png"><br/>

### 2.3 Transcriptional Changes in Intestinal Maf△Treg by Bulk RNA-seq

Bulk RNA-seq of intestinal Maf△Treg confirms the dysregulation of key genes, including *Il23r*, *Ctla4*, *Il10*, *Rorc*, *Pik3cd*, *Il17r*, and *Sh2d1a*. Hierarchical clustering reveals that genotype (WT vs KO) and tissue (gut vs spleen) are major determinants of transcriptional identity, with Maf△Treg exhibiting a partial loss of intestinal Treg signature.

## 3. Three-Dimensional Chromatin Organization in Tregs

### 3.1 CTCF-Mediated 3D Genome Organization at the Rorc Locus

Hi-C analysis (GSE274543) of Treg cells from spleen and lymph nodes reveals that CTCF-mediated 3D genome organization prevents exTreg differentiation. Contact maps at the *Rorc* locus (chr3:94,372,793–94,398,276) demonstrate that CTCF knockout alters chromatin looping and TAD boundaries, potentially permitting aberrant *Rorc* expression and exTreg conversion.

## 4. Single-Cell Profiling of T Cells in NOD Mice

### 4.1 T Cell Distribution Across Tissues

Single-cell RNA-seq of T cells across NOD mouse tissues (pancreas, pancreatic lymph node [pLN], and blood) reveals tissue-specific distribution patterns. UMAP embedding colored by tissue shows that pancreas and pLN harbor distinct T cell populations, while blood contains a mixture of circulating subsets.

**Single-Cell RNA-seq of T Cells Across NOD Mouse Tissues**<br/><img src="/images/Treg/Treg_5.png"><br/>

### 4.2 T Cell Subset Heterogeneity

UMAP embedding colored by T cell subset resolves conventional T cells (*Tcon_cm*, *Tcon_mem*, *Tcon_effmem*, *Tcon_eff*, *Tcon_prog*, *Tcon_Th21*, *Tcon_Tfh*), regulatory T cells (*Treg_resting*, *Treg_eff*), and other populations including *Acinar_contaminated*, *Recently_activated*, *Interferon_sensing*, and *Proliferating* cells. These subsets exhibit tissue-specific enrichment, with *Treg_eff* and *Tcon_eff* expanded in pancreas and pLN.

**T Cell Single-Cell Transcriptomes Across Tissues in NOD Mice**<br/><img src="/images/Treg/Treg_6.png"><br/>

### 4.3 Treatment-Dependent T Cell Composition

Stacked bar plots of T cell composition across control (IgG, Spt) and anti-PD-1 treatment reveal that anti-PD-1 induces substantial shifts in T cell subset proportions. *Tcon_mem* and *Tcon_effmem* expand under anti-PD-1, while *Treg_eff* and *Tcon_eff* fractions change, consistent with immune checkpoint blockade modulating T cell homeostasis.

## 5. TCR Clonal Expansion Across Tissues

### 5.1 Clonal Expansion by Tissue

Clonal expansion analysis reveals that the pancreas harbors the highest number of expanded clonotypes, followed by pLN and blood. This tissue-specific clonal architecture is consistent with local antigen-driven expansion in the pancreas during autoimmune diabetes.

**TCR Clonal Expansion Across Tissues**<br/><img src="/images/Treg/Treg_8.png"><br/>

### 5.2 Average Clonotype Size Across Tissues and Clusters

Average TCR clonotype size varies across tissues and Seurat clusters, with pancreas exhibiting larger average clonotype sizes than pLN and blood. CD8+ T cell clusters (*CD8_mem*, *CD8_effmem*, *CD8_eff*) and CD4+ T cell clusters (*Tcon_mem*, *Tcon_effmem*, *Treg_eff*) show distinct clonal expansion patterns, reflecting tissue-specific antigen encounter and differentiation.

## 6. Conclusion

Integration of bulk RNA-seq, ATAC-seq, Hi-C, and single-cell TCR profiling reveals a multilayered regulatory landscape of Tregs in intestinal tolerance and autoimmunity. c-Maf is essential for establishing the intestinal Treg epigenetic and transcriptional program, while CTCF-mediated 3D genome organization safeguards Treg stability. Single-cell profiling in NOD mice demonstrates tissue-specific T cell composition and clonal expansion, with anti-PD-1 treatment reshaping T cell homeostasis. These findings provide a framework for understanding Treg biology and identifying therapeutic targets for autoimmune diabetes.
