## Abstract

Innate lymphoid cells (ILCs) are developmentally derived from hematopoietic progenitors and acquire tissue-specific functional programs in peripheral organs. Recent advances in chromatin accessibility profiling (ATAC-seq), three-dimensional chromatin conformation capture (Hi-C), and single-cell transcriptomics have begun to resolve the epigenetic mechanisms that govern ILC lineage commitment and tissue adaptation. Here, we integrate data from *Cell* (2016), *Nat Immunol* (2015, 2018, 2023, 2024), *Sci Immunol* (2025), and *Cell Stem Cell* (2021) to examine the dynamic chromatin landscape of ILC development, with a focus on the role of NK receptors, transcription factors (TOX, GATA3, RORγt), and tissue-specific microenvironments.

## 1. Introduction

ILCs constitute a heterogeneous family of innate effector lymphocytes that mirror T helper cell subsets. They develop from common lymphoid progenitors (CLPs) in the bone marrow and fetal liver, and subsequently migrate to peripheral tissues where they acquire organ-specific functions. The molecular and epigenetic mechanisms governing these cell fate decisions remain incompletely understood. Recent multi-omic studies provide a high-resolution framework for dissecting ILC development, tissue imprinting, and 3D chromatin regulation.

## 2. Chromatin Accessibility Dynamics During ILC Development

### 2.1 ATAC-seq at Key Regulatory Loci

Chromatin accessibility at key regulatory loci undergoes dynamic changes during hematopoietic development, with distinct open chromatin states emerging in progenitor populations and maturing ILC subsets across multiple tissues. ATAC-seq data from GSE77695 (*Cell* 2016; *Nat Immunol* 2015) reveal that *TOX*, a transcription factor required for ILC progenitor development, exhibits differential accessibility across bone marrow HSCs, CLPs, ILC2Ps, and mature ILC subsets from gut, lung, and spleen.

## 3. Three-Dimensional Chromatin Organization in ILCs

### 3.1 Hi-C Reveals Lineage-Specific 3D Interactions

Hi-C analysis (Nat Immunol 2023) uncovers lineage-specific 3D chromatin interactions at key loci in developing and functional ILC subsets. Spleen NK cells (CD45+, CD3e−, NK1.1+, NKp46+) and bone marrow ILC2 (CD45+, Lin−, CD90.2+, ST2+) exhibit distinct topologically associating domain (TAD) structures and promoter–enhancer interactions that correlate with their transcriptional programs.

**Hi-C Contact Map at Locus in ILC Subsets**<br/><img src="/images/ILCDel/ILCDel_3.png"><br/>

### 3.2 Stimulus-Dependent 3D Remodeling at the Il5 Locus

Hi-C data (Nat Immunol 2024) reveal stimulus-dependent 3D chromatin remodeling at the *Il5* cytokine locus in lung ILC2s following NMU stimulation. This finding demonstrates that 3D genome organization is not static but dynamically regulated by environmental cues, providing a mechanism for rapid cytokine production.

## 4. Tissue-Specific Imprinting of ILC2

### 4.1 Tissue-Resident ILC2 Across Organs

Tissue-resident ILCs develop from bone marrow progenitors and acquire organ-specific molecular profiles across peripheral tissues. ILC2s isolated from skin, fat, gut, lung, and bone marrow (BAM) exhibit distinct transcriptional states, with tissue-specific expression of *Il18r1* and *Pdcd1*.

**UMAP of ILC2 Across Different Tissues**<br/><img src="/images/ILCDel/ILCDel_5.png"><br/>

### 4.2 Differential Gene Expression Across Tissues

Volcano plot analysis of lung, gut, and skin ILC2 versus bone marrow ILC2 (BAM) identifies tissue-specific gene signatures. Lung ILC2 upregulate *Cd2*, *Dusp2*, and *Nr4a1*; gut ILC2 upregulate *Ccr9*, *Il17rb*, and *Pmaip1*; skin ILC2 upregulate *Rgs18*, *Gimap8*, and *Vasp*. These gene changes reflect tissue-specific microenvironments that imprint distinct transcriptional programs.

**Volcano Plots of Tissue ILC2 vs Bone Marrow ILC2**<br/><img src="/images/ILCDel/ILCDel_6.png"><br/>

### 4.3 Functional Enrichment in Gut ILC2

Intestinal ILC2s exhibit a distinct functional signature characterized by activation of stress responses, cytokine production, and tissue remodeling pathways, reflecting their specialized adaptation to the gut microenvironment. GO enrichment analysis identifies pathways including regulation of protein modification, positive regulation of programmed cell death, IL-17 signaling, and response to unfolded protein.

## 5. Developmental Origins and Fetal Liver Hematopoiesis

### 5.1 Fetal Liver LTi Cells and IL-22 Potential

E15.5 Lin−CD45+CD127+Flt3−α4β7+CD25− lymphoid progenitors (αLPs) from fetal liver and bone marrow (Sci Immunol 2025) reveal that fetal liver LTi cells exhibit IL-22 transcriptional potential during early development, whereas ILC3s are largely absent in the adult liver. UMAP visualization and volcano plot analysis of FL versus BM ILCs identify *Il22* and *Rorc* as key differentially expressed genes.

**BM vs FL ILC UMAP with Il22 and Rorc Expression**<br/><img src="/images/ILCDel/ILCDel_8.png"><br/>

### 5.2 Human Fetal Liver Hematopoiesis and FLT3

Single-cell transcriptomics of human 17–22 pcw fetal liver and bone marrow (Cell Stem Cell 2021) identifies non-committed progenitors (Lin−CD34+CD38−) and their differentiation trajectories. *FLT3* (Fms-related tyrosine kinase 3) plays a pivotal role in the lineage commitment of multipotent progenitors (MPPs) during early hematopoiesis, with pseudotime analysis revealing a decline in *FLT3* expression as cells commit to ILC lineages.

## 6. Conclusion

Integration of ATAC-seq, Hi-C, and single-cell transcriptomics reveals a multilayered epigenetic landscape governing ILC development. Chromatin accessibility dynamics at *TOX* and *Gata3* loci, lineage-specific 3D chromatin interactions, and tissue-specific transcriptional imprinting together define ILC identity and function. The fetal liver–bone marrow axis and FLT3-dependent lineage commitment provide a developmental framework for understanding ILC heterogeneity. Future studies should focus on how 3D chromatin architecture integrates environmental signals to shape ILC fate decisions in health and disease.
