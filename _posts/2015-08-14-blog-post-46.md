## Abstract

Spinal cord injury (SCI) triggers a complex pathological cascade involving neuronal loss, glial activation, immune infiltration, and fibrotic scarring. Recent single-cell and spatial transcriptomic studies have begun to resolve the cellular heterogeneity and temporal dynamics of these processes. Here, we integrate data from mouse and human spinal cord atlases, injury time-course experiments, and developmental models to examine the molecular programs underlying SCI pathology. We highlight the emergence of mesenchymal and myofibroblast populations as key drivers of fibrosis, and discuss how developmental blueprints—such as NFIA-dependent chromatin remodeling in motor neurons—may inform strategies for promoting regeneration.

## 1. Introduction

SCI is a devastating condition characterized by irreversible loss of motor and sensory function. The secondary injury cascade involves neuroinflammation, excitotoxicity, glial scar formation, and fibrosis, which collectively create an inhibitory environment for axonal regeneration. Understanding the cellular and molecular events following SCI is essential for developing effective therapies. Recent advances in single-nucleus RNA-seq (snRNA-seq), single-cell RNA-seq (scRNA-seq), and spatial transcriptomics have provided unprecedented resolution of the SCI cellular ecosystem.

## 2. Single-Cell Atlases of Spinal Cord Injury

### 2.1 SnRNA-seq Time Course After T9 Injury

Using snRNA-seq (GSE172167), we characterized the dynamic cellular composition of mouse spinal cords at D0, D1, W1, W3, and W6 post-T9 injury. UMAP and composition analyses demonstrate progressive shifts in oligodendrocyte lineage cells and immune infiltration alongside neuronal changes. These data provide a direct cellular blueprint for understanding the pathological cascade and identifying therapeutic targets in SCI.<br/>

### 2.2 ScRNA-seq Time Course After SCI

Complementary scRNA-seq data (Signal Transduct Target Ther. 2022) extend the temporal resolution to D0, H4, D1, D3, D7, D14, and D38 post-injury. This dataset captures early inflammatory responses, peak immune infiltration, and the transition to chronic scar formation, providing a comprehensive view of the cellular dynamics underlying SCI.<br/>

## 3. Fibrosis and Mesenchymal Activation in SCI

### 3.1 Healthy Spinal Cord Atlas and Pro-Fibrotic Signaling

Single-cell transcriptomic landscape of the mouse spinal cord reveals expression of key fibrosis-related genes (*PDGFRB*, *TGFB1*, *TGFB2*). UMAP visualization of the human spinal cord cellular ecosystem identifies major populations including oligodendrocytes, astrocytes, neurons, immune cells, vascular cells, and blood cells. Feature plots show that *PDGFRB*, *TGFB1*, and *TGFB2* are predominantly expressed in oligodendrocytes and vascular/immune compartments, highlighting these cells as potential sources of pro-fibrotic signaling.<br/>

**Healthy Spinal Cord Atlas and Expression of Fibrosis-Related Genes**<br/><img src="/images/Injure/Injure_3.png"><br/>

### 3.2 Mesenchymal and Myofibroblast Markers

*COL1A1*, *FMOD*, *PDGFRA*, and *RGS5* are specifically expressed in mesenchymal cells and myofibroblasts, establishing a physiological baseline for SCI fibrosis research. These markers define the fibrotic cell populations that expand after injury and contribute to scar formation.<br/>

## 4. Sensory Neuron Diversity and Regeneration

DRG sensory neurons exhibit substantial transcriptional diversity, which may influence their regenerative capacity after SCI. Single-cell profiling of DRG neurons (Sci Transl Med. 2022) identifies distinct subtypes with differential expression of neurotrophic receptors and regeneration-associated genes.<br/>

## 5. Developmental Blueprints for Repair

### 5.1 Zebrafish scATAC-seq and Oligodendrocyte Precursor Formation

Single-cell ATAC-seq of zebrafish spinal cord cells at 36 hpf and 48 hpf post-fertilization identifies *gsx2* as a key regulator of oligodendrocyte precursor formation (Dev Biol. 2026, GSE316142). These findings highlight conserved developmental programs that may be reactivated or targeted for remyelination strategies.<br/>

**Zebrafish scATAC-seq Reveals *gsx2* as a Regulator of Oligodendrocyte Precursors**<br/><img src="/images/Injure/Injure_6.png"><br/>

### 5.2 NFIA Orchestrates Chromatin Accessibility in Motor Neurons

ATAC-seq data from E13.5 mouse spinal motor neurons (GSE268391) demonstrate that NFIA, a transcription factor regulating neural development and glial differentiation, orchestrates the chromatin landscape of developing motor neurons. Conditional knockout of NFIA alters accessibility at neural loci such as *Chat*. We hypothesize that recapitulating some developmental programs post-SCI will mitigate fibrotic scarring and enhance axonal regeneration.<br/>

## 6. Spatial and 3D Approaches to SCI

### 6.1 MERFISH Spatial Transcriptomics of Brain Fibroblast–Immune Interactions

MERFISH spatial transcriptomics of aging brain reveals that *PDGFRA* is a well-established marker for fibroblasts, identifying mesenchymal cells involved in tissue remodeling and repair (Nature 2025). TGFβ signaling mediates fibroblast–immune interactions, with distinct spatial distributions in healthy, subacute lesion, and chronic lesion states.<br/>

**MERFISH Spatial Mapping of PDGFRA and Fibroblast–Immune Interactions**<br/><img src="/images/Injure/Injure_8.png"><br/>

### 6.2 3D Spatial Reconstruction of Embryonic Development

3D spatial reconstruction of the E9.5 mouse embryo using ScanPy and Spateo (Cell 2025) provides a developmental blueprint for spinal cord formation. 3D transcriptomics reveals molecular holograms of mouse embryos, enabling the identification of key genes for neuron development, including NFIA.<br/>

**3D Spateo Reconstruction of E9.5 Mouse Embryo**<br/><img src="/images/Injure/Injure_9.png"><br/>

## 7. Conclusion

Integration of single-cell, spatial, and developmental transcriptomics reveals a multilayered landscape of SCI pathology. Mesenchymal and myofibroblast populations emerge as key drivers of fibrosis, while developmental programs—such as NFIA-dependent chromatin remodeling and *gsx2*-regulated oligodendrocyte precursor formation—provide a blueprint for regeneration. Future studies should focus on recapitulating these developmental programs to mitigate fibrotic scarring and promote functional recovery after SCI.
