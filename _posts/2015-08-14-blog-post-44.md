## Abstract

Innate lymphoid cells (ILCs) constitute a heterogeneous family of effector lymphocytes that orchestrate mucosal immunity, tissue homeostasis, and inflammatory responses. Their development and function are governed by a complex transcriptional network involving lineage-defining factors such as RORγt, T-bet, RORα, and Bcl6. Here, we integrate published ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, and single-cell RNA-seq data to examine how these transcription factors regulate key effector genes—particularly *Ifng* and *Bcl6*—across ILC subsets. The data reveal both cooperative and antagonistic interactions that shape ILC heterogeneity and functional plasticity.

## 1. Introduction

ILCs are innate counterparts of T helper cell subsets and are classified into cytotoxic NK cells, ILC1, ILC2, ILC3, and lymphoid tissue inducer (LTi) cells. Unlike adaptive lymphocytes, ILCs lack rearranged antigen receptors and rely on germline-encoded activating and inhibitory receptors. Their effector programs are tightly controlled by transcription factors that integrate environmental cues. Elucidating these transcriptional circuits is essential for understanding ILC biology in homeostasis and disease.

## 2. Chromatin Accessibility and Transcription Factor Binding at the *Ifng* Locus

### 2.1 ATAC-seq Profiling in ILC3

ATAC-seq data from *Nature Immunology* (2021) demonstrate that chromatin accessibility at the *Ifng* locus is dynamically regulated by RORγt and RORα. In `Id2^CreERT2` control cells, the *Ifng* promoter and upstream regulatory elements are accessible. However, in `Id2^iΔRORγt` and `Id2^iΔRORγt/RO Rα` ILC3, accessibility is markedly reduced, indicating that both RORγt and RORα are required to maintain an open chromatin state permissive for *Ifng* transcription.

### 2.2 ChIP-seq Evidence for Direct Binding

ChIP-seq tracks further reveal that RORγt binds to the *Ifng* locus in Th17 cells, whereas T-bet occupies distinct regulatory regions in Th1 cells. This suggests a model in which RORγt and T-bet compete for or differentially occupy *Ifng* regulatory elements, thereby dictating whether IFN-γ is repressed or activated. The data are consistent with the known antagonism between RORγt and T-bet in ILC3 and ILC1, respectively.

## 3. Single-Cell Transcriptomics of Bcl6 in ILC Subsets

### 3.1 ILC Clustering and Bcl6 Expression

Single-cell RNA-seq data from *Cell Reports* (2023) provide a comprehensive map of ILC subsets. UMAP visualization resolves ILC1, ILC2, ILC3, LTi-like ILC3, NK cells, and other lymphocytes. *Bcl6* expression is enriched in LTi-like ILC3 and a subset of ILC3, with lower expression in ILC2 and CD8+ T cells. A violin plot across sorted populations confirms that Bcl6 is highest in LTi-like ILC3, followed by ILC3 and NK/ILC1.<br/>

### 3.2 Functional Implications of Bcl6 in ILC3

To investigate Bcl6 function, `Rorc^Cre Bcl6^flox` mice were analyzed. Loss of Bcl6 in RORγt-expressing cells altered the expression of effector molecules (e.g., *Il17a*, *Il22*, *Osm*, *Lif*) and migration-related genes (e.g., *Ccr1*, *Ccl4*, *S1pr3*). These findings suggest that Bcl6 is not merely a marker but an active regulator of ILC3 effector and migratory programs.<br/>

## 4. Integrated Transcription Factor Network in ILCs

The data support a model in which:

- **RORγt** is essential for ILC3/LTi identity and maintains accessibility at *Ifng* and other effector loci.
- **T-bet** promotes ILC1/NK effector function and antagonizes RORγt at shared loci such as *Ifng*.
- **RORα** cooperates with RORγt to sustain chromatin accessibility and transcriptional output.
- **Bcl6** is enriched in LTi-like ILC3 and modulates effector molecule and chemokine receptor expression.

This network enables ILCs to adapt to local cues while preserving subset identity.

## 5. Conclusion

Integration of ATAC-seq, ChIP-seq, and single-cell transcriptomics reveals a multilayered transcriptional regulatory landscape in ILCs. The *Ifng* locus serves as a paradigm for RORγt/T-bet antagonism, while Bcl6 emerges as a key regulator of LTi-like ILC3 function. Future studies combining spatial transcriptomics and functional perturbations will further resolve how these transcription factors coordinate ILC responses in health and disease.
