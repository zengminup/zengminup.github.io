## Abstract

Pregnancy and lactation impose substantial metabolic demands on the maternal organism, requiring coordinated adaptations across multiple organ systems. Recent single-cell, spatial transcriptomic, and epigenetic studies have begun to resolve the molecular mechanisms underlying these adaptations. Here, we integrate data from *Cell* (2025) and *Nat Immunol* (2025) to examine hepatocyte metabolic remodeling and intestinal villus elongation during pregnancy and lactation. We highlight the central role of ILC3-derived IL-22 in driving crypt-villus hyperplasia and intestinal lengthening, and discuss the epigenetic regulation of hepatocyte function by STAT3 and H3K27ac. These findings reveal a coordinated inter-organ axis that meets the elevated nutrient demands of reproduction.

## 1. Introduction

Reproduction requires profound physiological adaptations to support fetal growth and milk production. The maternal liver undergoes metabolic reprogramming to meet increased energy demands, while the small intestine elongates and remodels its villus architecture to enhance nutrient absorption. The molecular and cellular mechanisms coordinating these adaptations remain incompletely understood. Recent multi-omic studies provide a high-resolution framework for dissecting the hepatic and intestinal adaptations to pregnancy and lactation.

## 2. Metabolic Remodeling in Ileal Epithelial Cells

### 2.1 Single-Cell Profiling Across Virgin, Lactation, and SGLT3a KO

Single-cell RNA-seq of ileal epithelial cells (Cell: 134010; *Cell* 2025) reveals substantial transcriptional remodeling across virgin, lactating, and SGLT3a knockout lactating mice. Enterocytes, goblet cells, Paneth cells, enteroendocrine cells (EEC), and stem cells exhibit distinct gene expression programs, with lactating mice showing expanded enterocyte populations and altered metabolic gene expression. SGLT3a KO partially reverses these changes, implicating sodium-glucose transport in the lactational adaptation.

**Single-Cell Profiling of Ileal Epithelial Cells Across Virgin, Lactation, and SGLT3a KO**<br/><img src="/images/pregnancy/pregnancy_2.png"><br/>

### 2.2 Spatial Distribution of Epithelial Genes

Spatial transcriptomics (Xenium, 3,410,863 cells) reveals the spatial organization of epithelial gene expression across virgin, pregnant, and lactating ileum. Genes including *Lyz1*, *Fabp6*, *Fabp2*, *Epcam*, and *Il22* exhibit distinct spatial distributions, with villus tips and crypts showing differential expression patterns.

## 3. Spatial Transcriptomics of Intestinal Adaptation

### 3.1 Xenium Spatial Mapping Across Reproductive States

Xenium spatial transcriptomics of virgin (V), pregnant (P), and lactating (L) ileum resolves distinct cell populations and their spatial organization. UMAP embedding and feature plots reveal expression of *Alpi*, *Fabp1*, *Apoa1*, *Muc2*, *Tff3*, *Dclk1*, *Trpm5*, *Lgr5*, *Olfm4*, *Chga*, and *Il22* across epithelial and immune compartments.

**Xenium Spatial Transcriptomics of Virgin, Pregnant, and Lactating Ileum**<br/><img src="/images/pregnancy/pregnancy_4.png"><br/>

### 3.2 Cell Type Composition and Gene Expression

Dot plot analysis of marker genes across V, P, and L conditions reveals differential expression of *Fabp1*, *Fabp2*, *Apoa4*, *Apoa1*, *Apoa3*, *Pdia6*, *Slc10a2*, *Slc51a*, *Slc51b*, *Slc10a1*, and *Slc10a2*. Lactating ileum exhibits expanded enterocyte populations and increased expression of lipid metabolism genes, consistent with enhanced nutrient absorption.

## 4. Epigenetic Regulation of Hepatocyte Function

### 4.1 STAT3 and H3K27ac in Pregnant Hepatocytes

CUT&RUN data (*Cell* 2025) reveal that hepatocytes undergo epigenetic and metabolic alterations during pregnancy. STAT3 binding and H3K27ac enrichment at the *Egr1* locus are increased in wild-type pregnant hepatocytes, whereas GP130 KO and ACSS2 KO hepatocytes exhibit reduced STAT3 binding and H3K27ac enrichment at the *Stat3* locus. These findings identify STAT3 and ACSS2 as key regulators of hepatocyte metabolic adaptation.

**CUT&RUN Tracks of STAT3 and H3K27ac at the Egr1 and Stat3 Loci**<br/><img src="/images/pregnancy/pregnancy_5.png"><br/>

### 4.2 Mammary Gland Single-Cell Profiling

Single-cell RNA-seq of mammary gland (*Nat Immunol* 2025) reveals dynamic changes in epithelial, lymphoid, myeloid, fibroblast, and endothelial populations across germ-free (GF) and specific-pathogen-free (SPF) virgin and lactating mice. *Ahr* expression is enriched in SPF lactating mammary gland, implicating aryl hydrocarbon receptor signaling in mammary gland immunity.

## 5. Hormone-Associated Intestinal Adaptation

### 5.1 Spatial Distribution of Esr1

Spatial transcriptomics reveals that *Esr1* (estrogen receptor 1) exhibits distinct spatial distributions across virgin, pregnant, and lactating ileum. *Esr1* expression is enriched in specific villus regions, suggesting hormone-dependent regulation of intestinal adaptation.

**Spatial Distribution of Esr1 in Virgin, Pregnant, and Lactating Ileum**<br/><img src="/images/pregnancy/pregnancy_7.png"><br/>

### 5.2 ILC3 in Hormone-Associated Intestinal Adaptation

ILC3-derived IL-22 plays a central role in hormone-associated intestinal adaptation during pregnancy and lactation, driving crypt-villus hyperplasia and intestinal lengthening. The androgen–ILC2–dendritic cell axis in skin immunity (*Science* 2024) provides a parallel example of sex hormone–immune crosstalk.

## 6. Transgenerational Chromatin Remodeling

### 6.1 Maternal Immune Activation and CD4+ T Cell Epigenetics

ATAC-seq of naïve CD4+ T cells from spleen and lymph nodes reveals that maternal immune activation (MIA) and stool transfer (ST) induce transgenerational chromatin remodeling. Experimental groups include PBS (control), MIA, and ST, with ATAC-seq assessing chromatin accessibility at *Stat5a*, *Stat3*, and *Gm40295* loci.

## 7. Conclusion

Integration of single-cell, spatial, and epigenetic data reveals a coordinated inter-organ axis of maternal adaptation during pregnancy and lactation. Hepatocytes undergo STAT3- and ACSS2-dependent metabolic reprogramming, while intestinal epithelial cells expand and remodel under the influence of ILC3-derived IL-22 and estrogen signaling. Transgenerational chromatin remodeling in CD4+ T cells further highlights the lasting epigenetic impact of maternal immune challenges. These findings provide a framework for understanding how reproduction reshapes maternal physiology and offspring immunity.
