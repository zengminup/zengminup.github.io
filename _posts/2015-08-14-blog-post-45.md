## Abstract

The enteric nervous system (ENS) constitutes a complex network of neurons and glia that regulates gastrointestinal motility, secretion, and immune homeostasis. Recent advances in single-cell transcriptomics, ATAC-seq, and spatial transcriptomics have begun to resolve the cellular heterogeneity of the ENS and its interactions with immune cells, particularly type 2 innate lymphoid cells (ILC2). Here, we integrate data from *Science* (2025), *Immunity* (2017), *Cell* (2025), and *bioRxiv* (2025) to examine region-specific ENS subtypes, neuroreceptor expression, and spatial organization in homeostasis and disease. These findings highlight the emerging role of neuroimmune circuits—such as the VIP–VIPR1 and CALCRL–CGRP axes—in gut physiology and pathology.

## 1. Introduction

The gastrointestinal tract is innervated by the ENS, which comprises diverse neuronal subtypes that sense and respond to microbial, dietary, and inflammatory cues. Beyond classical neurotransmission, enteric neurons communicate with immune cells, including ILC2, macrophages, and T cells, thereby shaping mucosal immunity. However, the molecular identity and spatial organization of these neuroimmune interactions remain incompletely understood. Recent multi-omic studies provide a high-resolution framework for dissecting these circuits.

## 2. Region-Specific ENS Heterogeneity

### 2.1 Single-Cell Atlas of the ENS

Data from *Science* (2025) present a region-specific single-cell atlas of the ENS across germ-free (GF), *Bacteroides thetaiotaomicron* (BT), altered Schaedler flora (ASF), specific-pathogen-free (SPF), and ovalbumin (OVA)-treated mice. UMAP embedding resolves distinct enteric neuron subtypes, including sensory neurons, secretomotor neurons, vasomotor neurons, inhibitory motor neurons, and excitatory motor neurons. Notably, the atlas captures both regional (ileum vs. colon) and condition-dependent transcriptional states.

### 2.2 Condition-Dependent Neuronal States

Comparison across conditions reveals that microbial colonization and type 2 inflammation (OVA/OVAs) induce substantial transcriptional remodeling of enteric neurons. Volcano plot analysis of ASF versus SPF identifies differentially expressed genes in specific neuronal subtypes (e.g., PSN Nmu-hi), including neuropeptides and receptors involved in sensory and immune crosstalk.

## 3. Neuroreceptor Expression and Chromatin Accessibility

### 3.1 ATAC-seq at Neuroreceptor Loci

ATAC-seq data from *Immunity* (2017) reveal that gut ILC2 and IL-25/IL-33–stimulated ILC2 exhibit distinct chromatin accessibility profiles at neuroreceptor loci, including *Calcrl* (CGRP receptor 1) and *Tacr1* (tachykinin receptor 1). These findings suggest that ILC2 are poised to respond to neuropeptides such as CGRP and substance P, providing a molecular basis for neuroimmune crosstalk.

**ATAC-seq Tracks at *Calcrl* and *Tacr1* Loci in ILC2**<br/><img src="/images/gutneuron/gutneuron_3.png"><br/>

### 3.2 Transcription Factor Motifs and Neuroreceptor Pathways

Motif enrichment analysis at these accessible regions identifies binding sites for transcription factors involved in ILC2 identity and activation, including RORα, GATA3, and NF-κB. Together with the VIP–VIPR1 pathway, these data delineate a neuroreceptor repertoire that enables ILC2 to integrate neuronal signals.

## 4. Spatial Organization of Neuroimmune Interactions

### 4.1 Gut Cell Atlas and Merfish Mapping

A comprehensive gut cell atlas from *bioRxiv* (2025) integrates single-cell and spatial transcriptomics (Merfish) to map enteric neurons, immune cells, and epithelial cells in germ-free and colonized mice. Spatial mapping of *Nmur1* and *Chat* reveals distinct anatomical distributions of neuronal subtypes relative to immune and epithelial compartments.

**Gut Cell Atlas and Spatial Mapping of Enteric Neurons**<br/><img src="/images/gutneuron/gutneuron_4.png"><br/>

### 4.2 Neuronal Subtypes and Neuropeptide Expression

Single-cell data from *Cell* (2025) further resolve enteric neuron subtypes and their expression of *Nos1*, *Nmu*, and *Vip*. These neuropeptides exhibit subtype-specific and spatially restricted expression patterns, consistent with specialized roles in motility, secretion, and immune modulation.

**Enteric Neuron UMAP with *Nos1*, *Nmu*, and *Vip* Expression**<br/><img src="/images/gutneuron/gutneuron_5.png"><br/>

## 5. VIP–VIPR1 Axis in Homeostasis and Disease

Spatial transcriptomics of skin (as a model of peripheral neuroimmune interactions) demonstrates that VIP and VIPR1 are spatially co-organized in wild-type tissue, whereas disease states exhibit altered distribution and increased expression in immune-rich regions. These findings suggest that the VIP–VIPR1 axis may serve as a conserved neuroimmune module across barrier tissues.

## 6. Conclusion

Integration of single-cell transcriptomics, ATAC-seq, and spatial transcriptomics reveals a multilayered landscape of enteric neuroimmune interactions. Region-specific ENS subtypes, neuroreceptor expression in ILC2, and spatial organization of neuropeptide–receptor axes together provide a framework for understanding how the nervous system regulates mucosal immunity. Future studies combining functional perturbations with spatial multi-omics will further elucidate the role of neuroimmune circuits in gastrointestinal health and disease.
