## Abstract

Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic inflammatory demyelinating disease of the central nervous system characterized by focal lesions, glial activation, and immune infiltration. Recent advances in spatial transcriptomics have enabled high-resolution mapping of cellular composition and molecular signatures within MS lesions. Here, we integrate MERFISH and Xenium in situ data from human white matter (GSE284005) to examine the spatial organization of neurons, oligodendrocytes, astrocytes, vascular cells, lymphocytes, and microglia/macrophages in MS and control tissue. We further highlight the spatial expression of key molecules including AQP4, CD19, and BTK, which mark astrocytic, B cell, and microglial/macrophage compartments, respectively.

## 1. Introduction

MS is driven by autoimmune-mediated demyelination and neurodegeneration, with lesions characterized by blood–brain barrier disruption, immune cell infiltration, and reactive gliosis. Understanding the spatial organization of distinct cell types within lesions is essential for elucidating disease mechanisms and identifying therapeutic targets. Spatial transcriptomics technologies—including MERFISH and Xenium—provide single-cell resolution while preserving tissue architecture, enabling direct interrogation of cellular neighborhoods in situ.

## 2. MERFISH Spatial Transcriptomics of MS White Matter

### 2.1 Cellular Composition in MS vs Control

MERFISH spatial transcriptomics of human white matter (GSE284005) resolves major cell populations including neurons, oligodendrocytes, vascular cells, astrocytes, lymphocytes, oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs), and microglia/macrophages. Comparison of MS lesions with healthy control tissue reveals substantial shifts in cellular composition, with expansion of immune and glial populations in MS.

### 2.2 Spatial Reorganization of Glial and Immune Compartments

A second MERFISH panel confirms the spatial reorganization of glial and immune compartments in MS lesions. Oligodendrocytes and OPCs exhibit distinct distribution patterns relative to astrocytes and microglia/macrophages, consistent with demyelination and remyelination attempts.

## 3. Spatial Expression of Disease-Relevant Molecules

### 3.1 AQP4 in Astrocytic Compartments

AQP4, the astrocytic water channel and target of neuromyelitis optica autoantibodies, exhibits widespread spatial expression in MS white matter, with enrichment in perivascular and subpial regions. AQP4 expression overlaps with astrocyte-rich areas, consistent with reactive astrogliosis.

**Spatial Expression of AQP4 in MS White Matter**<br/><img src="/images/sclerosis/sclerosis_3.png"><br/>

### 3.2 CD19 in B Cell Compartments

CD19, a pan-B cell marker, is spatially restricted to lymphocyte clusters within MS lesions, consistent with the presence of B cell aggregates and meningeal follicles. CD19+ cells are enriched in perivascular and subarachnoid spaces.

**Spatial Expression of CD19 in MS White Matter**<br/><img src="/images/sclerosis/sclerosis_4.png"><br/>

### 3.3 BTK in Microglia/Macrophage Compartments

BTK (Bruton's tyrosine kinase), a key signaling molecule in B cells and myeloid cells, exhibits spatial expression in microglia/macrophage-rich regions of MS lesions. BTK is a therapeutic target of interest in MS, and its spatial distribution supports a role in innate immune activation.

**Spatial Expression of BTK in MS White Matter**<br/><img src="/images/sclerosis/sclerosis_5.png"><br/>

## 4. Xenium In Situ Profiling of Human MS

### 4.1 UMAP of Cell Types in MS and Control

Xenium in situ profiling resolves major cell types in human MS and control tissue, including astrocytes, astrocytes_DA (disease-associated), endothelial/vascular cells, ependymal cells, immature oligodendrocytes, myeloid populations (Myeloid_1, Myeloid_2), neurons, oligodendrocytes (OLG), white matter oligodendrocytes (OLG_WM), Schwann cells, vascular cells, and vascular/immune cells.

**Xenium UMAP of Cell Types in Human MS**<br/><img src="/images/sclerosis/sclerosis_6.png"><br/>

### 4.2 Sample-Level Heterogeneity

UMAP embedding colored by sample ID (MSa1, MSa2, CTRL1, CTRL2, MSi2, MSi1) reveals that samples broadly overlap, indicating shared cell type identities across conditions, while also highlighting sample-specific differences in cell state abundance.

### 4.3 Spatial Distribution of Cell Types

Spatial distribution plots reveal the anatomical organization of cell types across MS and control tissue sections. Astrocytes and oligodendrocytes occupy distinct white matter domains, while immune and vascular cells localize to perivascular and lesional regions. These spatial patterns provide a framework for understanding lesion architecture.

## 5. Conclusion

Integration of MERFISH and Xenium spatial transcriptomics reveals the cellular and molecular architecture of MS lesions with unprecedented resolution. Distinct spatial distributions of AQP4, CD19, and BTK highlight astrocytic, B cell, and microglial/macrophage contributions to MS pathology. These findings support the development of spatially targeted therapies and provide a foundation for understanding lesion heterogeneity in MS.
